This week & recent

What's moving right now

Developments from the last few weeks that change what HR should be doing.

  • 16 Jul 26

    Government pushes back the harassment-duty start date to 30 October

    An updated ERA implementation timeline moves the strengthened "all reasonable steps" sexual-harassment duty and the new third-party harassment liability from 1 October to 30 October 2026 — now landing alongside the union access regime. The tribunal time-limit extension (3 → 6 months) and right-to-work check changes are unaffected and still start 1 October.

    Do: you have four extra weeks for harassment risk assessments and training — but keep tribunal and right-to-work prep on the original 1 October date.
  • 6 Jul 26

    Trade union Codes of Practice laid before Parliament

    Revised draft codes on the new right of workplace access, on recognition/derecognition conduct, and on time off for union duties were laid before Parliament alongside secondary legislation, ahead of the 30 October commencement. The access code confirms access is the default outcome once a union asks, backed by CAC enforcement and penalties up to £500,000.

    Do: draft your access-request response process now, using the code's timelines as your template.
  • 17 Jul 26

    Cross-Channel seafarers' pay and rest consultation closes

    Proposals would guarantee seafarers on frequent UK–France and Channel Islands crossings pay equivalent to at least the National Minimum Wage for the whole voyage, plus statutory minimum rest periods. Government is targeting a December 2026 start for the new Mandatory Seafarers' Charter.

    Do: if you operate or charter cross-Channel ferry or freight routes, start planning crew pay and rest rotas ahead of the expected December start.
  • 30 Jun 26

    Holiday-pay enforcement consultation open — closes 22 Sep

    DBT's consultation would give the Fair Work Agency power to investigate holiday-pay compliance up to six years back, take a "whole employer" approach across a workforce, and issue civil penalties — enforcement expected to begin in 2027.

    Do: pressure-test your holiday pay calculations and records now — enforcement is coming, not just record-keeping.

Legislation radar

Employment Rights Act 2025 — the rollout

Royal Assent 18 Dec 2025; provisions commence in waves. Green is in force, amber is next, grey is further out.

  • 18 FEB 2026 In force

    Industrial action reforms

    Strike notice cut 14 → 10 days, ballot mandates extended 6 → 12 months, new protection from dismissal for industrial action.

  • 6 APR 2026 In force

    The April package

    SSP from day one with the lower earnings limit removed · day-one paternity & unpaid parental leave · collective-redundancy protective award doubled to 180 days' pay · six-year holiday pay record-keeping duty · Fair Work Agency established · whistleblowing protections widened.

  • 31 AUG 2026 —

    Electronic balloting

    E-balloting and workplace voting for industrial action ballots go live (subject to parliamentary approval); repeal of the 50% turnout threshold expected (timing to be confirmed).

  • 1 OCT 2026 —

    The October package

    Tribunal time limits 3 → 6 months · workers must be told of their right to join a union · expanded right-to-work checks. Combined with the 12-week Acas Early Conciliation window (live since Dec 2025; government reviews whether it stays at 12 weeks in October 2026), some claims could reach nine months start-to-finish.

  • 30 OCT 2026 —

    Union access rights & harassment duty

    Access regime live for employers with 21+ employees; agreed-terms breaches attract penalties up to £500k. Also from this date (delayed from 1 Oct): the "all reasonable steps" duty to prevent sexual harassment, and liability for third-party harassment across all protected characteristics.

  • LATE 2026 Ahead

    Autumn stragglers

    Fair pay agreement process launches in adult social care; tipping reforms rescheduled from October to end of 2026; Mandatory Seafarers' Charter targeted for December (NMW-equivalent pay and rest for cross-Channel crew).

  • 1 JAN 2027 Ahead

    The January cliff

    Unfair dismissal qualifying period drops to six months and the compensatory cap goes · fire-and-rehire becomes automatically unfair (incl. varying pay, pensions, hours, shifts, holidays) · replacing an employee with a non-employee becomes automatically unfair · new "organisation-wide" collective-redundancy consultation threshold (pushed back from Oct 2026).

  • 2027 Ahead

    Through 2027

    Bereavement leave incl. pregnancy loss before 24 weeks · zero-hours guaranteed-hours contracts and shift notice · NDAs banned in harassment/discrimination cases · tighter flexible-working refusal test · mandatory gender equality action plans · FWA starts enforcing holiday pay.

  • 2028+ Ahead

    On the horizon

    Ethnicity and disability pay gap reporting — first snapshot unlikely before April 2028, reporting from 2029. Employment status review and TUPE reform still pending.

Case law watch

Recent decisions

  • XX v YY

    EAT: duress is a relevant factor when judging whether conduct amounts to gross misconduct — a tribunal must weigh the employee's circumstances, not just the act itself, before summary dismissal is fair.

  • Bank of Africa UK v Hassani

    Who employs a secondee decides who carries whistleblowing liability — keep secondment paperwork matching day-to-day reality.

  • Cunningham v BBC

    "Constructive knowledge" of disability can arise without a formal diagnosis — act on the impact you can see, not the label.

  • Leedham v Royal Mail Group Ltd

    EAT: giving a payslip doesn't require a paper copy — reasonable, unrestricted digital access satisfies the duty, provided staff without easy access aren't disadvantaged.

  • London Ambulance Service v Garrett

    A protected belief doesn't protect every statement; comments need a close nexus to the belief itself.

  • Chandrashekarappa v Wipro Ltd

    EAT: once a discretionary bonus is approved, employers can't retrospectively "move the goalposts" with a cap — set final terms before you confirm an award.
