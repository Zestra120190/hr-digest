This week & recent

What's moving right now

Developments from the last few weeks that change what HR should be doing.

  • 30 Jul 26

    Ex-Connells chief wins age discrimination case over a 46p share payout

    A Bury St Edmunds tribunal found Skipton Group unlawfully treated former Connells CEO David Livesey, 67, as a "bad leaver" on exit after 33 years, cancelling £420,000 of shares for a 46p cheque while a 57-year-old former colleague got £1.6m for a smaller stake — his unfair dismissal claim also succeeded, though bullying was dismissed; a remedy hearing follows in October.

    Do: if "good/bad leaver" share terms are applied differently to departing staff of different ages, check the reasoning is documented and consistent — a stark payout gap is exactly the comparator evidence age claims are built on.
  • 30 Jul 26

    Acas launches consultation on a revised disciplinary & grievance Code of Practice

    Acas has opened a consultation on updates to the Code that tribunals use to judge whether dismissals and grievances were handled fairly — non-compliance can already move a tribunal award up or down by 25%. It closes 23 September, alongside Acas's separate review of the trade union time-off Code for the Employment Rights Act.

    Do: read the draft Code when published and flag anything that would force changes to your disciplinary and grievance procedure — this Code carries real weight on tribunal compensation.
  • 29 Jul 26

    Supreme Court: Home Office must say exactly why it's fining you over a right-to-work breach

    The Court unanimously quashed a £15,000 illegal-working penalty against Akbars restaurants because the notice listed all three possible statutory grounds for a breach without identifying which one applied — fairness requires the Home Office to decide and state its reason so an employer can judge whether to challenge it. It lands as Immigration Enforcement's illegal-working visits rose 57.5% in 2025.

    Do: if you get a civil penalty notice, check it names the specific statutory ground relied on — a notice that just lists the possibilities can be quashed on that basis alone.
  • 29 Jul 26

    Rail operators hit with record ~£5m penalty for bypassing union pay talks

    The EAT upheld LNER and Hitachi Rail's liability for writing directly to around 1,235 RMT members with a pay offer while talks with the recognised union were still live — confirming an award of £3,907 per claimant, near £5m in total and over £6m with interest, the largest penalty yet under TULRCA s.145B.

    Do: if pay talks with a recognised union are still live, don't go around them with a direct offer to members — tribunals look past your stated reasons to your real purpose for making the offer.
  • 16 Jul 26

    "Volunteer" coastguard was really a worker — and government abandons its own pay-cut plan

    The Court of Appeal confirmed in Maritime and Coastguard Agency v Groom that a coastguard rescue officer engaged as a "volunteer" for 35 years was a worker for each attendance where remuneration was available, entitling him to NMW and holiday pay — mutuality of obligation outside a specific callout wasn't needed. Days after MPs piled pressure on in Parliament, the Department for Transport confirmed its plan to move Coastguard Rescue Officers to an expenses-only model from September 2026 will no longer go ahead.

    Do: if "volunteers" receive any payment tied to their time — even discretionary call-out or expenses-badged sums — review whether that has created worker status; relabelling the arrangement doesn't undo it.

Legislation radar

Employment Rights Act 2025 — the rollout

Royal Assent 18 Dec 2025; provisions commence in waves. Green is in force, amber is next, grey is further out.

  • 18 FEB 2026 In force

    Industrial action reforms

    Strike notice cut 14 → 10 days, ballot mandates extended 6 → 12 months, new protection from dismissal for industrial action.

  • 6 APR 2026 In force

    The April package

    SSP from day one with the lower earnings limit removed · day-one paternity & unpaid parental leave · collective-redundancy protective award doubled to 180 days' pay · six-year holiday pay record-keeping duty · Fair Work Agency established · whistleblowing protections widened.

  • 25 AUG 2026 —

    Electronic balloting

    Final Code of Practice published 28 July confirms e-voting and hybrid voting go live for industrial action, political fund and union election/merger ballots — six days earlier than the 31 August originally trailed; workplace voting also permitted for industrial-action ballots with employer consent. Repeal of the 50% turnout threshold expected (timing tbc); recognition/derecognition ballots follow in 2027.

  • 1 OCT 2026 —

    The October package

    Tribunal time limits 3 → 6 months · workers must be told of their right to join a union · expanded right-to-work checks. Combined with the 12-week Acas Early Conciliation window (live since Dec 2025; government reviews whether it stays at 12 weeks in October 2026), some claims could reach nine months start-to-finish.

  • 30 OCT 2026 —

    Union access rights & harassment duty

    Access regime live for employers with 21+ employees; agreed-terms breaches attract penalties up to £500k. Also from this date (delayed from 1 Oct): the "all reasonable steps" duty to prevent sexual harassment, liability for third-party harassment, and a new right not to suffer detriment for taking industrial action — deducting strike-day pay stays lawful, but a detriment failure now joins the list for a 25% Acas uplift.

  • LATE 2026 Ahead

    Autumn stragglers

    Fair pay agreement process launches in adult social care; tipping reforms rescheduled from October to end of 2026; Mandatory Seafarers' Charter targeted for December (NMW-equivalent pay and rest for cross-Channel crew).

  • 1 JAN 2027 Ahead

    The January cliff

    Unfair dismissal qualifying period drops to six months and the compensatory cap goes · fire-and-rehire becomes automatically unfair (incl. varying pay, pensions, hours, shifts, holidays) · replacing an employee with a non-employee becomes automatically unfair · new "organisation-wide" collective-redundancy consultation threshold (pushed back from Oct 2026).

  • 2027 Ahead

    Through 2027

    Bereavement leave incl. pregnancy loss before 24 weeks · zero-hours guaranteed-hours contracts and shift notice · NDAs banned in harassment/discrimination cases · tighter flexible-working refusal test · mandatory gender equality action plans · FWA starts enforcing holiday pay.

  • 2028+ Ahead

    On the horizon

    Ethnicity and disability pay gap reporting — first snapshot unlikely before April 2028, reporting from 2029. Employment status review and TUPE reform still pending.

Case law watch

Recent decisions

  • Livesey v Skipton Group (Connells)

    Employment tribunal (Bury St Edmunds): treating a 67-year-old former CEO as a "bad leaver" — cancelling £420,000 of shares for a 46p cheque, against £1.6m paid to a 57-year-old former colleague for a smaller stake — was age discrimination, and the exit process was a constructive unfair dismissal; bullying was dismissed. Remedy hearing due October. Apply leaver classifications consistently, whatever the departing employee's age.

  • Akbars Restaurant (Middlesbrough) v Secretary of State for the Home Department

    Supreme Court [2026] UKSC 26: unanimously quashed a £15,000 right-to-work civil penalty because the notice listed all three statutory grounds for a breach without saying which one applied — an employer is entitled to know the state's actual reason before deciding whether to appeal. Never let a penalty notice's stated basis go unchecked.

  • Wickham v HMRC

    Employment tribunal: a trainee investigator awarded £155,077 — including £42,000 for injury to feelings — after senior staff colluded to build a case against him once he disclosed depression, anxiety and PTSD-type symptoms; the panel called it one of the most shocking cases of systemic bullying it had seen. A disclosure must trigger support, not scrutiny.

  • London North Eastern Railway & Hitachi Rail v Jiwanji & others

    EAT: upheld a record ~£5m penalty (£3,907 per claimant, c.1,235 RMT members) for writing directly to unionised staff with a pay offer while talks with their recognised union were still live — a breach of TULRCA s.145B. Negotiate through the union's process, don't go around it.

  • Maritime and Coastguard Agency v Groom

    Court of Appeal [2026] EWCA Civ 6: a coastguard rescue officer engaged as a "volunteer" for 35 years was a worker for each period of attendance where remuneration was available — the right to be paid, once documented, was a binding contract even without mutuality of obligation outside a specific callout. Paying "volunteers" for their time can create worker rights whatever the label says.
