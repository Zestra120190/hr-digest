This week & recent

What's moving right now

Developments from the last few weeks that change what HR should be doing.

  • 23 Jul 26

    Lawyers urge employers to bolster dismissal & redundancy procedures ahead of the compensation cap removal

    New analysis of HMRC data by TWM Solicitors finds around 840,000 people earned more than the current £123,543 unfair dismissal compensation cap in 2025/26 — that cap disappears entirely on 1 January 2027, alongside the drop to a six-month qualifying period, sharply raising the stakes on any dismissal that goes wrong.

    Do: tighten up dismissal, redundancy and probation-exit paperwork now — documented, consistent reasoning matters far more once awards are uncapped.
  • 22 Jul 26

    Pay awards hold at 3.3% for a third straight quarter as CPI eases to 2.6%

    Brightmine's analysis of 268 employers' April–June settlements (3.6m employees) puts the median pay award at 3.3%, unchanged for a third rolling quarter, while ONS CPI fell to 2.6% in the year to June — a real-terms gap widening further below the 3.8% Sept-2025 CPI benchmark used for April's statutory-rate uprating.

    Do: benchmark any autumn pay review against the 3.3% median, not last year's figures — most settlements are clustering 3–4%, comfortably above current CPI.
  • 15 Jul 26

    Home Office updates right-to-work guidance ahead of the 1 October expansion

    New guidance clarifies how the widened right-to-work regime — covering agency workers, subcontractors, gig and platform-based labour — will operate in practice, including when civil liability for a missing check can extend beyond the direct employer along a labour supply chain.

    Do: map your labour supply chain now — contracts, governance and identity-check evidence need to show compliance before 1 October, not just for direct employees.
  • 10 Jul 26

    Fair Work Agency's proposed holiday-pay enforcement powers take shape

    The Department for Business and Trade's consultation (closing 22 September) sets out how the FWA could enforce statutory holiday pay from 2027 — investigating underpayments, recovering arrears for workers and issuing penalties of up to 200% of arrears capped at £20,000 per worker, taking a "whole employer" view rather than waiting for individual complaints; cited research found 56% of employers pay all types of holiday at the same flat rate, a common compliance trap for irregular-hours and part-year staff.

    Do: audit how holiday pay is actually calculated now, especially for irregular-hours and part-year workers — the FWA's proactive powers won't wait for someone to complain.
  • 08 Jul 26

    Consultation closes on new restrictions for harassment and discrimination NDAs

    The government's consultation on regulations under ERA s.24 — which will void NDA clauses gagging workers from disclosing relevant harassment or discrimination, unless a narrow "excepted agreement" test is met (independent legal advice, informed written consent, a cooling-off period) — closed on 8 July; the change is expected in 2027 and won't apply retrospectively.

    Do: start reviewing standard settlement-agreement confidentiality wording now — blanket harassment/discrimination gag clauses won't survive without the new excepted-agreement safeguards.

Legislation radar

Employment Rights Act 2025 — the rollout

Royal Assent 18 Dec 2025; provisions commence in waves. Green is in force, amber is next, grey is further out.

  • 18 FEB 2026 In force

    Industrial action reforms

    Strike notice cut 14 → 10 days, ballot mandates extended 6 → 12 months, new protection from dismissal for industrial action.

  • 6 APR 2026 In force

    The April package

    SSP from day one with the lower earnings limit removed · day-one paternity & unpaid parental leave · collective-redundancy protective award doubled to 180 days' pay · six-year holiday pay record-keeping duty · Fair Work Agency established · whistleblowing protections widened.

  • 31 AUG 2026 —

    Electronic balloting

    Order and Code of Practice laid 22 June: e-voting and hybrid voting go live for industrial action, political fund and union election/merger ballots; workplace voting also permitted for industrial-action ballots with employer consent. Repeal of the 50% turnout threshold expected (timing tbc); recognition/derecognition ballots follow in 2027.

  • 1 OCT 2026 —

    The October package

    Tribunal time limits 3 → 6 months · workers must be told of their right to join a union · expanded right-to-work checks. Combined with the 12-week Acas Early Conciliation window (live since Dec 2025; government reviews whether it stays at 12 weeks in October 2026), some claims could reach nine months start-to-finish.

  • 30 OCT 2026 —

    Union access rights & harassment duty

    Access regime live for employers with 21+ employees; agreed-terms breaches attract penalties up to £500k. Also from this date (delayed from 1 Oct): the "all reasonable steps" duty to prevent sexual harassment, liability for third-party harassment, and a new right not to suffer detriment for taking industrial action — deducting strike-day pay stays lawful, but a detriment failure now joins the list for a 25% Acas uplift.

  • LATE 2026 Ahead

    Autumn stragglers

    Fair pay agreement process launches in adult social care; tipping reforms rescheduled from October to end of 2026; Mandatory Seafarers' Charter targeted for December (NMW-equivalent pay and rest for cross-Channel crew).

  • 1 JAN 2027 Ahead

    The January cliff

    Unfair dismissal qualifying period drops to six months and the compensatory cap goes · fire-and-rehire becomes automatically unfair (incl. varying pay, pensions, hours, shifts, holidays) · replacing an employee with a non-employee becomes automatically unfair · new "organisation-wide" collective-redundancy consultation threshold (pushed back from Oct 2026).

  • 2027 Ahead

    Through 2027

    Bereavement leave incl. pregnancy loss before 24 weeks · zero-hours guaranteed-hours contracts and shift notice · NDAs banned in harassment/discrimination cases · tighter flexible-working refusal test · mandatory gender equality action plans · FWA starts enforcing holiday pay.

  • 2028+ Ahead

    On the horizon

    Ethnicity and disability pay gap reporting — first snapshot unlikely before April 2028, reporting from 2029. Employment status review and TUPE reform still pending.

Case law watch

Recent decisions

  • XX v YY

    EAT: a head teacher summarily dismissed over messages sent under a coercive, controlling relationship had her case sent back to tribunal — duress at the time of the misconduct is a relevant factor in whether it justifies summary dismissal, so it can't be left out of a gross-misconduct assessment. Give investigations room to hear the full context, not just the act itself.

  • Harber v Intrinsic Care Group

    Employment tribunal: withdrawing a job offer after the candidate disclosed a breast cancer diagnosis was direct disability discrimination — nearly £17,000 awarded. Never let a health disclosure between offer and start date change your mind about a hire.

  • Steward v Langford Village Community Primary School

    Employment tribunal: a reference marking a whistleblowing site manager down across the board — after earlier internal emails had praised his work — was found to be an unlawful whistleblowing detriment; £33,000 awarded. Keep references consistent with contemporaneous performance records.

  • Tarbuc v Martello Piling Ltd

    EAT: a "protected conversation" under s.111A only shields ordinary unfair dismissal claims — and any hint of impropriety (no notice, no chance to bring a companion) puts the whole protection back in play, including for wages and discrimination claims.

  • Chandrashekarappa v Wipro Ltd

    EAT: once a sector lead had approved a discretionary bonus worth over £500,000 under the scheme's published terms, Wipro couldn't retrospectively impose a fresh cap and sign-off that cut the payout to £112,000 — the entitlement had already crystallised and was reclaimed as an unlawful deduction from wages. Document bonus discretion before you exercise it, not after.

  • Leedham v Royal Mail Group Ltd

    EAT: giving employees ongoing access to itemised payslips through an app or web portal satisfies the duty to be "given" a pay statement — it needn't be a paper document, provided access isn't blocked by cost or technical barriers. Digital-only payslips are lawful, but keep a paper fallback for anyone who genuinely can't access them.
